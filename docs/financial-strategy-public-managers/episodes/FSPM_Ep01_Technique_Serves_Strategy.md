## Transcript

A spreadsheet is not a strategy. Public financial management starts by naming the public result, the constraints, and the decision that financial evidence must support. Sharon Kioko and Justin Marlowe organize Financial Strategy for Public Managers around a practical claim: technique serves strategy. Forecasts, statements, ratios, cost models, and budgets matter because they improve choices.

This episode adapts the book's overview, published by University of Washington Pressbooks under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 except where otherwise noted. The narration condenses, paraphrases, and reorganizes the authors' explanatory prose. Separately credited cases, examples, figures, tables, photographs, quotations, and linked resources are excluded.

Public organizations do not optimize a single bottom line. A city may need to preserve access, meet legal duties, maintain service quality, protect resilience, and use public money fairly at the same time. Financial analysis makes those tradeoffs visible. It cannot decide whose needs count or how risk should be shared. Those are governance choices, and the analysis should expose rather than conceal them.

Begin with a decision statement. Name the service outcome, the people affected, the time horizon, and the authority responsible. Then distinguish recurring resources from one-time resources, controllable costs from obligations, and measured facts from assumptions. A projection should show a base case and credible alternatives instead of presenting one precise future.

A useful recommendation connects mission and money. It explains what changes operationally, identifies cash and budget effects, tests sensitivity to uncertain inputs, and names what evidence would trigger a course correction. The same reasoning should be understandable to finance staff, program managers, elected officials, and residents.

Torrance application segment. This original independent-study segment is separate from Kioko and Marlowe's book. It cites three City of Torrance primary-source pages checked October seventh, twenty twenty-six: Budget Documents at torrance C A dot gov slash Government slash Departments Offices slash Finance slash Budget Documents; Audit Reports at the same Finance section slash Audit Reports; and Transparent Torrance at torrance C A dot gov slash Government slash Transparent Torrance.

For a hypothetical service proposal, begin with the adopted budget and current budget-review records to identify authority and planned resources. Use the annual comprehensive financial report listed on the Audit Reports page to establish audited historical context. Use the transparency page only as a route to current dashboards and tools, with each platform and reporting period labeled. Then state the proposed public result, alternatives, assumptions, and monitoring trigger. This workflow does not evaluate Torrance's financial condition or recommend a City decision.

The discipline is repeatable: define the public purpose, assemble relevant evidence, compare feasible choices, explain distributional effects, decide, and monitor. Technical fluency supports accountability only when the public can follow the argument.
