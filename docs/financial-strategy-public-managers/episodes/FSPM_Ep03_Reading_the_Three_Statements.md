## Transcript

Three financial statements answer different questions. The statement of financial position describes resources and obligations at a date. The operating statement describes revenues and expenses over a period. The cash-flow statement explains why cash changed.

This episode adapts Kioko and Marlowe's chapter The Basic Financial Statements, published by University of Washington Pressbooks under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 except where otherwise noted. It condenses, paraphrases, and reorganizes the authors' explanatory prose. Separately credited cases, examples, figures, tables, quotations, and linked materials are excluded.

Financial position is a stock. Assets represent controlled resources, liabilities represent obligations, and net position is the residual. Classification matters because cash available for current bills is not the same as a restricted asset or a capital asset used to provide services.

The operating statement is a flow. Under accrual accounting, revenue and expense recognition follow economic activity rather than only the movement of cash. Depreciation allocates the cost of a long-lived asset across periods. Receivables and payables connect current activity to cash collected or paid later.

Cash flow restores the timing view. Operating activity, capital investment, and financing can move cash in different directions. An organization may report a favorable operating result while cash falls because it acquires equipment or repays debt. It may gain cash through borrowing without improving its underlying operating balance.

Government reporting may also present more than one measurement focus. Government-wide statements emphasize the broader economic position, while fund statements organize legally and operationally distinct resources. A reader should identify the reporting entity, basis of accounting, period, and fund before comparing numbers. Reconciliations explain why two valid views differ.

For a hypothetical review, select one annual comprehensive financial report from the official index and record its fiscal year before opening it. Identify the government-wide statements, major-fund statements, cash-flow statements where applicable, notes, and reconciliations. For every observation, preserve the statement name, reporting unit, date, and accounting basis. Do not combine a citywide net-position figure with a single-fund cash balance. This exercise teaches document navigation and reaches no conclusion about Torrance's financial health.

Read the statements together and compare multiple periods. Ask whether reported results are recurring, whether liquid resources cover near-term claims, whether capital assets are being maintained, and whether liabilities are shifting costs forward. Notes and accounting policies are part of the statements, not optional fine print.
