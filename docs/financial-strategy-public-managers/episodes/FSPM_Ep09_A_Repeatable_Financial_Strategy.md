## Transcript

Financial strategy is a cycle linking mission, evidence, choice, implementation, and learning. It is not a final spreadsheet or a once-a-year budget exercise.

This episode synthesizes the book overview and the chapters Financial Statement Analysis, Cost Analysis, and Budget Strategy by Sharon Kioko and Justin Marlowe. University of Washington Pressbooks publishes them under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 except where otherwise noted. The narration condenses, paraphrases, and reorganizes the authors' explanatory prose. Third-party cases, examples, figures, tables, quotations, and linked resources are excluded.

Start with the service outcome and decision authority. Establish financial position, cash timing, legal constraints, and the current operating path. Map revenue restrictions and concentration. Measure full cost, then isolate the costs and revenues that change under each feasible alternative.

Test demand, inflation, collection timing, implementation capacity, and downside risk. Compare the distribution of benefits and burdens. A preferred option should remain understandable when assumptions change, and the recommendation should identify the assumptions most likely to alter the choice.

Translate the decision into a budget, cash plan, responsibilities, and a small monitoring dashboard. State thresholds for correction before results arrive. Preserve the source data, calculations, definitions, and rejected alternatives so another analyst can reproduce the work.

Use a decision file with five parts. First, define the outcome, authority, population, and horizon. Second, preserve the audited baseline and current budget. Third, state alternatives with full and incremental costs. Fourth, record uncertainty, distributional effects, and implementation constraints. Fifth, set measures, owners, reporting dates, and correction thresholds. Version the file so changed assumptions remain visible.

Reconcile periods and reporting units before analysis. Define two feasible alternatives and a current-policy baseline. Compare service effects, cash timing, recurring balance, full cost, marginal cost, risk, and who bears benefits and burdens. Publish assumptions and a trigger for reconsideration. This workflow makes no judgment about Torrance's financial condition and offers no recommendation on an actual City service.

Communicate the result as a public argument. Explain uncertainty, opportunity cost, and tradeoffs in plain language. Kioko and Marlowe's central lesson is the guide: technical work earns its value when it improves strategy, accountability, and the organization's ability to deliver its mission.
