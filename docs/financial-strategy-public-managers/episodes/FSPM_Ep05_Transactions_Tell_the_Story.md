## Transcript

Financial statements are built from transaction rules. Understanding recognition and timing helps a manager see why the budget, cash balance, and accrual statements can all be correct while showing different results.

This episode adapts Kioko and Marlowe's chapter Accrual Accounting, published by University of Washington Pressbooks under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 except where otherwise noted. It condenses, paraphrases, and reorganizes the authors' explanatory prose. Separately credited cases, examples, figures, tables, quotations, and linked materials are excluded.

The accounting equation keeps each transaction balanced. Buying equipment for cash changes the composition of assets. Buying it on credit adds an asset and a liability. Paying the liability later reduces cash and the obligation. The economic event and the cash event can occur in different periods.

Accrual accounting recognizes revenue when earned under the applicable rules and expense when resources are consumed or obligations arise. Receivables, payables, prepaid items, and deferred inflows or outflows carry timing across reporting periods. Depreciation recognizes the service potential consumed from capital assets.

Budgetary accounting serves legal control. Appropriations authorize spending, and encumbrances reserve authority for commitments such as purchase orders. Neither mechanism is identical to an accrual expense. Reconciliation is therefore a management task, not evidence that one view is wrong.

Cutoff is a practical discipline. At period end, managers should ask whether goods were received, services were performed, eligibility requirements were met, invoices were pending, or collections related to another period. The answer affects payables, receivables, deferrals, expenses, and budget reports differently. A documented event timeline is more reliable than guessing from the check date.

Torrance application segment. This original local exercise is separate from the textbook. It cites the City of Torrance Budget Documents and Audit Reports pages, checked October seventh, twenty twenty-six, in the Finance section of torrance C A dot gov under Budget Documents and Audit Reports.

For a hypothetical capital purchase, construct a timeline with authorization, commitment, delivery, acceptance, invoice, payment, and placement in service. Identify which event affects an appropriation, an encumbrance, cash, a payable, a capital asset, and later depreciation. Then locate the relevant adopted-budget and audited-reporting records through the official indexes. This is a teaching workflow, not a statement about a particular Torrance transaction or accounting treatment.

For any surprising result, trace the underlying events. Ask what was ordered, delivered, accepted, billed, collected, capitalized, or paid, and under which reporting boundary. A transaction trail makes forecasts better because it reveals which timing differences will reverse and which indicate a lasting change.
