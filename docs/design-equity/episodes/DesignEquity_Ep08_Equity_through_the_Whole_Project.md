## Transcript

An equitable idea can become inequitable through procurement, engagement, construction, operations, maintenance, or evaluation. Equity has to travel through the entire project, from the first definition of need to the long-term distribution of results.

This episode adapts Kristine Miller's Discipline-Specific Professional Design Processes and Equity chapter under CC BY 4.0. It condenses and paraphrases Miller's work and excludes third-party readings, examples, images, quotations, and linked media.

At initiation, ask who requested the project and who was absent. At scope, define equity outcomes and decision authority. At procurement, examine team capacity, community relationships, accessibility, and the resources assigned to engagement. At design, compare distributional effects. At implementation, manage disruption and access. During operation, fund maintenance. During evaluation, ask affected people whether the result works.

Trust cannot be scheduled as one public meeting. People need time, accessible choices, honest limits, and evidence that participation mattered. Community-rooted organizations may hold relationships a project team lacks, but partnership should not mean extracting trust without resources or shared authority.

Create an equity control plan. For each project phase, name the decision, responsible person, affected groups, engagement method, evidence, success measure, and correction trigger. Add budget lines for language access, disability access, participation support, and evaluation.

Use triggers rather than promises. If access falls below a target, if displacement indicators rise, if maintenance is unfunded, or if a burden concentrates in one place, specify who must respond and by when.

Torrance application segment. This is original independent study, separate from Miller's chapter. It cites the City of Torrance General Plan page, checked October seventh, twenty twenty-six.

For a hypothetical implementation program in the Land Use or Safety Element, write an equity control plan from policy adoption through capital budgeting and annual reporting. Identify which actions are City-controlled and which require another agency. Link every outcome to a responsible department, funding path, schedule, and public report.

Include a correction process if benefits do not reach intended users or burdens emerge. This exercise does not describe an adopted Torrance program, determine compliance, or provide legal advice.

The full-course takeaway is that equity is a continuous design responsibility. Ask who benefits, who is burdened, who decides, whose knowledge counts, what history matters, and how results will be corrected.
