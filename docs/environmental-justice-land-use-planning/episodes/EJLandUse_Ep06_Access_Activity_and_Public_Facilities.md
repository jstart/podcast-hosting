Environmental justice includes reducing harmful exposure and increasing access to conditions that support health. Public facilities, physical activity, healthy food, safe housing, mobility, parks, and civic participation form a connected system.

This independent lesson draws from California DOJ's Best Practices for Implementing SB one thousand and current Government Code section sixty-five thousand three hundred two. Verify current law and local facts. This is not legal advice.

Presence is not access. A park can be nearby but separated by a dangerous crossing. A facility can exist but lack accessible hours, language support, shade, or disability access. A food outlet can be close but unaffordable. Evaluate the complete path between a person and the benefit.

Use five access dimensions: proximity, affordability, safety, usability, and reliability. Add quality and capacity where evidence permits. Measure different times and travel modes. Identify who maintains the facility and whether operation is funded.

Avoid a single citywide average. Compare neighborhoods and populations at a useful scale while protecting privacy. Combine network analysis with direct observation and community accounts. If a dataset counts an asset but residents report it is unusable, investigate the discrepancy.

Coordinate policies. Land use can reserve sites and permit uses. Capital budgets build facilities. Operations budgets keep them open. Transportation connects people. Housing policy affects whether intended beneficiaries can remain nearby.

Choose one hypothetical public facility or health-supporting destination. Map a realistic walking, rolling, transit, and driving trip from several residential areas. Audit crossings, shade, seating, schedule, accessibility, and cost. Ask users what the map misses.

Develop one land-use action, one capital action, and one operational action, each with an agency and measure. Do not claim the exercise describes existing inequity or determines legal compliance in Torrance.

Access is a chain. An equitable plan must connect location, transportation, affordability, design, operation, and housing stability so that an apparent benefit becomes a usable one.
