## Transcript

Identifying environmental-justice communities is both a data task and a duty of attention. A threshold can reveal a pattern, but it can also hide people whose burdens occur at a smaller scale, through a different indicator, or through conditions not represented in the tool.

This independent study uses California DOJ's Best Practices for Implementing SB one thousand and current Government Code section sixty-five thousand three hundred two. It is educational preparation, not legal advice.

The DOJ guide describes more than one route for identifying disadvantaged communities under current law. A real analysis must use the operative statute and authoritative current data. Do not collapse the routes into one score or assume that one screening tool decides the legal question.

Use a layered baseline. First map pollution and environmental burdens. Second examine socioeconomic and health vulnerability using appropriate, privacy-protective data. Third gather local evidence about routes, timing, indoor conditions, cumulative stressors, and community boundaries. Fourth review planning and investment history.

Document dataset date, geography, missing values, uncertainty, and scale. Census tracts are analytical units, not complete communities. Conditions near a road, facility, or parcel may differ within one tract.

Create a methods log before looking at results. State the statutory pathway being evaluated, data sources, thresholds, supplemental evidence, and who reviewed the method. Test sensitivity to reasonable changes. If the conclusion changes, report that instead of choosing the preferred output.

Engage residents before boundaries are fixed. Ask how people name their community and which conditions the data miss. Protect individuals from re-identification.

Torrance application segment. This independent exercise cites the City of Torrance General Plan page, checked October seventh, twenty twenty-six.

Prepare a hypothetical screening memo for the planning area using current authoritative data, but label it exploratory. Compare more than one geography and include a data-gap section. Invite correction through accessible outreach. Do not announce that Torrance has or lacks a legally defined disadvantaged community based on this exercise.

Any real determination requires current law, complete local facts, the City's official process, and qualified legal advice.

Good identification work makes burdens visible without pretending that people are points on a map. It combines lawful methods, transparent data, local knowledge, and humility.
