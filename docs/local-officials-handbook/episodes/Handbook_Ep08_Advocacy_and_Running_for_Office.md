## Transcript

Most advocates show up at the hearing, which is the moment of least influence. By the time the agenda packet is posted, staff's recommendation is set, and you are asking elected officials to break from it. The highest-leverage moments come weeks earlier.

Think of it in five steps. First, talk to the project planner or department head while the item is still being framed, and ask what would change the recommendation. Second, before the staff report is final, send a dated letter with a specific request. Third, meet decision-makers one at a time, and never carry one member's position to another, because that is how advocates create illegal serial meetings. Fourth, give public comment that adds something new, and follow up in writing. Fifth, after the decision, watch the follow-through.
The research on public comment is sobering. A study of thousands of comments at Massachusetts planning meetings found that commenters were older, more often homeowners, and far more opposed to new housing than voters as a whole. Decision-makers know the room is unrepresentative, which is exactly why organized testimony from renters, young families, and workers carries weight.

If an issue might end up in court, raise it before the decision, in writing. A lawsuit over a land use decision is limited to issues raised at the hearing or in letters delivered before it, and environmental lawsuits have a similar rule. And the Public Records Act gives you documents within ten days, plus one short extension. Name the department, a date range, and search terms.

Running for office in Torrance starts with the calendar. Council Districts 2, 4, and 6 are up on March seventh, 2028. The mayor and Districts 1, 3, and 5 are up in June 2030. School board Areas A and B are up in November 2028. City races file with the Torrance City Clerk. School board races file with the Los Angeles County Registrar.
Before you raise or spend a dollar, file a statement of intention with the state's campaign agency, and open one campaign bank account. Once you raise or spend two thousand dollars, you register a committee within ten days. Then come regular reports, plus twenty-four hour reports for large contributions in the final ninety days.
Torrance sets its own contribution limit: one thousand dollars per person per candidate per election cycle, with your own loans capped at thirty thousand dollars. School board races have no state limit. And remember the Levine Act. If you run from an advisory commission, you are not restricted during the campaign, but once seated you must step aside on council matters whose parties gave you more than five hundred dollars in the prior year. Screen your donors against council agendas before you accept.
Finally, keep the campaign separate from the city. No city staff, email, phones, or offices for campaign work.

The scale is small. The 2026 mayor's race was decided by about twelve hundred votes out of roughly forty-three thousand. A council district is about a sixth of the city. Races that size reward direct voter contact: knocking on doors, neighborhood coffees, and endorsements from people who know your work.
For more, see the advocacy and running for office sections of the Local Official's Handbook.
