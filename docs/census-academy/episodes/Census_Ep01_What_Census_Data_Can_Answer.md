## Transcript

This episode focuses on program choice. Census data becomes useful when the population, measure, geography, and time period are stated before anyone searches for a number.

Start with a question, not a table. Define the people or organizations, measure, geography, and time period before selecting a Census program. Population counts, household characteristics, housing conditions, business activity, and government finance come from different collections.

Write the statistical universe in ordinary language. A value might describe residents, households, occupied housing units, workers, establishments with paid employees, or governments. A percentage without its denominator can answer a different question than the one a decision maker asked.

Record the program, dataset, vintage, table or variable identifier, geography, and retrieval date. Keep survey estimates separate from administrative counts and preserve margins of error when supplied. The record is part of the finding.
