## Transcript

This episode focuses on ACS period estimates. Census data becomes useful when the population, measure, geography, and time period are stated before anyone searches for a number.

ACS one-year and five-year products measure periods of different length. A one-year estimate uses twelve months of collected data and favors currency. A five-year estimate combines sixty months, offers broader geographic coverage, and generally favors precision.

The five-year label names the final year, but the estimate does not describe that date alone. It describes the entire collection period. Do not call it a rolling average unless the specific statistic and method justify that language.

Compare like products and nonoverlapping periods when possible. A one-year estimate and a five-year estimate are not direct substitutes. Product choice should follow geography, precision needs, and the decision's tolerance for older observations.
