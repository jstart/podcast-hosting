## Transcript

This episode focuses on data products. Census data becomes useful when the population, measure, geography, and time period are stated before anyone searches for a number.

Detailed tables provide the most variables and include estimates and margins of error. Subject tables organize selected measures around a topic. Data profiles summarize characteristics, and comparison profiles emphasize change.

A table identifier communicates product and concept. Read the title, universe, annotation symbols, and variable labels before downloading. Filters in data dot census dot gov should be saved or documented so another person can repeat the search.

Profiles are useful for orientation, but a policy claim may require the underlying detailed table. Export machine-readable data when calculations matter, and preserve an untouched copy of the download.
