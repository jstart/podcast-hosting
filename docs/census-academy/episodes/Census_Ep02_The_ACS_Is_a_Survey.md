## Transcript

This episode focuses on survey foundations. Census data becomes useful when the population, measure, geography, and time period are stated before anyone searches for a number.

The American Community Survey is a continuous sample survey, not a complete annual count. It produces period estimates about social, economic, housing, and demographic characteristics. The estimate describes data collected across the stated period.

Sampling makes uncertainty measurable. Nonresponse, question wording, editing, weighting, and coverage also affect interpretation. The published estimate and margin of error belong together, while technical documentation explains the methods behind them.

Before using a variable, read its concept, label, universe, and year. Similar labels can apply to different populations. An ACS estimate should not be substituted for an administrative total merely because both concern the same topic.
