## Transcript

This episode focuses on economic programs. Census data becomes useful when the population, measure, geography, and time period are stated before anyone searches for a number.

Business statistics describe establishments, firms, industries, employment, payroll, and receipts under program-specific rules. County Business Patterns covers establishments with paid employees and reports annual subnational data by industry and size.

An establishment is a location where business activity occurs, while a firm may control multiple establishments. Nonemployer Statistics covers many businesses without paid employees. Economic Census and annual programs have different frequency and detail.

Industry classifications, suppression, reference years, and geographic coverage can change. Do not combine business counts with ACS worker estimates as if they share a universe. Use each program for the question it was designed to answer.
