## Transcript

This episode focuses on statistical uncertainty. Census data becomes useful when the population, measure, geography, and time period are stated before anyone searches for a number.

Every ACS estimate is accompanied by a margin of error at the stated confidence level. The margin helps express sampling uncertainty and should travel with the estimate in tables, charts, and decision memos.

A larger estimate is not necessarily statistically different from a smaller one. Use the Census Bureau's statistical testing guidance rather than judging overlap by eye. Derived percentages and sums need correctly calculated uncertainty.

Small groups and small geographies often have wide intervals. Suppress unstable rankings, combine categories only when substantively valid, or use a larger geography or longer period. Precision should determine the strength of the claim.
